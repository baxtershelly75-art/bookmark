# Exodus — source record

This file is the durable source trail for the Exodus 1–40 Living Bookmark build on branch `exodus-heart-study`.

## Governing rules

- Scripture leads. Chapter cards are built from Exodus in context and use explained cross-references where later Scripture directly clarifies or reuses the passage.
- **Margin notes = heart. Study Notes = education. Relevance, not quota.**
- Historical commentary uses a short exact excerpt with an identified author, a chapter-specific source URL, and a separate plain-English explanation.
- Historical voices are deliberately rotated so the book does not default to one or two writers.
- Pastor Brian material was not forced into this pass. His preaching may be used later only when a specific verified sermon genuinely illuminates an Exodus passage, with the video source and timestamp recorded.
- Difficult passages are documented rather than smoothed over: Pharaoh’s hardening, the firstborn judgment, sea judgment, slavery/service laws, Exodus 21:22–25, Canaanite judgment, the golden calf, Levite judgment, and the limits of tabernacle typology.
- Tabernacle symbolism follows explicit canonical connections (especially Hebrews, the Gospels, Psalms, and Revelation) rather than assigning a secret meaning to every material or measurement.

## Key canonical source trails used repeatedly

- Covenant continuity: Genesis 12; 15; 17; 46; 50.
- Passover and Christ: 1 Corinthians 5:7; Matthew 26:28; John 19:36.
- Sea and wilderness: 1 Corinthians 10:1–5.
- Sinai people-language: 1 Peter 2:9; Hebrews 12:18–24.
- Law/context guardrails: Deuteronomy 24:16; Ezekiel 18:20; Matthew 5:38–44.
- Tabernacle / priesthood / covenant: Hebrews 7–10.
- Incense and prayer: Psalm 141:2; Revelation 8:3–4.
- Moses’ veil: 2 Corinthians 3:7–18.
- God’s self-revelation in Exodus 34:6–7: Psalm 103:8; Jonah 4:2 and other Old Testament echoes.
- Final dwelling arc: John 1:14; Revelation 21:3.

## Interpretation guardrails recorded in the cards

- `Exodus 3`: the Angel of the LORD / pre-incarnate Christ identification is presented as a historic Christian reading, not as an explicit statement of the verse.
- `Exodus 6:3`: the divine-name question is acknowledged rather than pretending Genesis never uses YHWH.
- `Exodus 7–10`: Pharaoh’s self-hardening and God’s hardening are held together; plague-vs-Egyptian-deity charts are not presented as inspired one-to-one identifications.
- `Exodus 12`: Christ/Passover connection follows explicit New Testament warrant.
- `Exodus 13–14`: Yam Suph geography is left appropriately uncertain; the text’s theological claim does not depend on one modern map pin.
- `Exodus 15:26`: “I am the LORD that healeth thee” is not turned into a promise that faithful Christians will never be ill.
- `Exodus 21`: ancient servitude is neither equated simplistically with modern race-based chattel slavery nor sanitized into ordinary employment.
- `Exodus 21:22–25`: the pregnancy-injury translation dispute is stated plainly; no modern bioethical argument is treated as resting on an uncontested rendering.
- `Exodus 23`: Canaanite judgment is treated as a specific covenant-historical judgment, not a modern holy-war authorization.
- `Exodus 25–40`: Christ-centered/tabernacle connections are made where Scripture gives warrant, without a one-to-one symbolism code.
- `Exodus 32`: the Levite judgment is not used as a model for Christian violence.
- `Exodus 33`: “face to face” and “cannot see my face” are explained as different modes/degrees of revelation.
